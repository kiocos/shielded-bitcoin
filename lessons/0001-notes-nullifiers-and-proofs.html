How can a hidden spend be checked?

Your goal: explain why hiding a note does not let its owner spend it twice.

Note

A private value record: (v, d, rseed) means amount, receive-path diversifier, and fresh randomness. The chain carries encrypted output data. Replay derives a public tree leaf from it.

Nullifier

A public spend marker, deterministically tied to one note and its position. Spending that same note again produces the same marker. Replay rejects repeats.

ZK proof

Evidence that the hidden inputs and outputs obey the transfer rules. Indexers verify it without receiving the private notes, spend secret, or which leaves were spent.

Primary source: Shielded Bitcoin §§5, 9 and 14.

What the proof connects

“I know notes in this anchored tree and their spending secret. These are their correct nullifiers. The new ciphertexts encrypt the outputs I am proving about. All amounts are in range, and total input value equals total output value.”

The proof also binds the transfer body. The prover uses private inputs called a witness; the verifier receives public inputs and the proof. The arithmetic is checked without revealing those private inputs.

nf = H_nf(sk_nf, ρ, pos)

sk_nf is derived from the spending secret inside the circuit; ρ comes from the note’s randomness; pos is its replay-assigned tree position. The spender cannot freely substitute any of these while proving the same note.

Two checks work together: the proof establishes that a nullifier is correct; replay checks whether it has already been used. A membership proof alone does not establish that a note remains unspent. §14.2

How much privacy does “zero knowledge” promise here?

The proof does not expose the private witness. The complete transaction still exposes metadata such as timing, input/output counts and fee funding. Cryptographic privacy is not a promise that outside information can never identify a payment. The proof-system and trusted-setup assumptions matter too. §§17 and 20
