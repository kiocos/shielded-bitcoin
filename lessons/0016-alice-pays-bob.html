M08 · Core course · about 10 minutes

Follow one payment from wallet to accepted state.

Alice spends a 10,000-sat note, sends 6,000 to Bob and creates 4,000 in change.

You’ll be able to: Narrate a payment without confusing wallet intent, Bitcoin confirmation and shielded acceptance.

Start with an existing spendable note

Assume entry into the system has already happened. Alice’s wallet knows an accepted note, its position and the secrets needed to spend it. Bob gives her a shielded address. This example demonstrates a transfer; it does not supply the missing peg specification.

Alice synchronizes with current replayed state, checks spent status and selects an allowed anchor containing her input. She obtains the input’s membership path under that root.

Fix the transfer before making its proof

Alice creates Bob’s output and her change output with fresh randomness. She derives the input nullifier, encrypts the notes and constructs the outgoing recovery record. These fields determine the canonical body and its binding hash.

Only then does she produce a proof for that exact body and selected root. The public shape is one input and two outputs. Observers do not learn these three private amounts from the proof.

Publication, acceptance and receipt

A Bitcoin carrier publishes the envelope and pays its own fee. Inclusion lets indexers process it in Bitcoin order. If every replay check passes, the input nullifier is inserted and the two output leaves receive positions.

Bob can detect the output using his viewing capability and verify the recovered record against accepted state. Decryption by itself is not acceptance. Spending the new note requires a later allowed anchor containing it; the base rule does not allow same-block spending. Wallet confirmation policy may require additional depth.
