01

Foundations and the problem

Explain Bitcoin’s public ledger, the proof vocabulary, and who checks each part of Shielded Bitcoin

M01Bitcoin’s public ledger7 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Read a small Bitcoin transaction and separate public facts from ownership guesses.

Topics: UTXOs, transaction inputs and outputs, change, signatures, fees, confirmation, and observable payment relationships.

Practice: Label the public amounts and links in a payment with change, then identify which ownership claims require extra evidence.

Suggested background: no prior knowledge assumed.

Reading: Bitcoin Developer Guide: transactions Introduction and transaction structure; Shielded Bitcoin paper §§1–2.

M02The cryptographic vocabulary8 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Choose the right primitive for hiding data, committing to data, authorizing a spend, or proving a condition.

Topics: Hashing, commitments, encryption and signatures; a first distinction between hiding data and proving a claim.

Practice: Assign a primitive to four protocol tasks and explain what each one does not establish.

Suggested background: M01.

Reading: Shielded Bitcoin paper §§3, 5 and 14; Bitcoin PIPEs v2 §2.

F01Statements, witnesses and checking rules5 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Describe a claim and its evidence before using cryptographic notation.

Topics: Public inputs, claims, witnesses, relations, and the separate proof object.

Practice: Construct and check a subset totaling 31, then distinguish a private transfer witness from its public proof.

Suggested background: no prior knowledge assumed.

Reading: Cook: The P versus NP Problem §1; Shielded Bitcoin paper §14.

F02P, NP and the open question6 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Explain NP without claiming it means hard or non-polynomial.

Topics: Decision problems, polynomial time, efficient witness checks, P contained in NP, and what remains unproved.

Practice: Compare combinations with checking one witness, and explain why brute-force growth is not a lower bound.

Suggested background: F01.

Reading: Cook: The P versus NP Problem §§1–2.

F03What zero knowledge adds5 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Explain how proof validity and witness privacy are separate properties.

Topics: Prover, verifier, completeness, soundness, privacy relative to public inputs, and encryption versus proof.

Practice: Compare sending private evidence, merely asserting validity, and sending a zero-knowledge proof.

Suggested background: F01, F02.

Reading: Thaler: Proofs, Arguments, and Zero-Knowledge §§3.1–3.2 and 11.1; Shielded Bitcoin paper §§14 and 20.

F04SNARKs, circuits and constraints8 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Expand SNARK accurately and explain how payment rules become proof constraints.

Topics: Succinctness, non-interactivity, arguments, knowledge, zk-SNARKs, constraints, Groth16 and trusted setup.

Practice: Check balanced amounts with negative change to discover why balance needs range checks.

Suggested background: F03.

Reading: Groth: On the Size of Pairing-based Non-interactive Arguments abstract and construction; Shielded Bitcoin paper §17 and Appendices A–B.

M03The complete system map7 min
Open lesson →

You’ll be able to: Place each responsibility with the component that performs it.

Topics: Wallets, Bitcoin publication and ordering, replaying indexers, shielded state, and entry/exit boundaries.

Practice: Sort actions among wallet, Bitcoin, indexer and an unspecified peg component.

Suggested background: M01, M02, F04.

Reading: Shielded Bitcoin paper §§1.2, 3–4.

Stage checkpoint: Explain a statement, witness, proof and zk-SNARK in plain English, then assign publication, transfer checks and key release to the correct components.

M10Deterministic replay and indexer trust9 min

Topics: Parsing, body binding, anchor checks, nullifier checks, proof verification, atomic updates, note tree, nullifier set, root history and accepted history.

Suggested background: M05, M10.
