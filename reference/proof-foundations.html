The words behind the proof.

Plain-English definitions for reading the Shielded Bitcoin and PIPEs papers.

Statement
A precise claim about public information.
Witness
Data that satisfies the claim’s checking rule. It is not inherently private.
Relation
The rule for checking a public input together with a proposed witness.
NP statement
A claim with a witness of polynomially bounded length that can be checked in polynomial time.
P / NP
P: efficiently decidable yes-or-no problems. NP: yes-instances have efficiently checkable witnesses. P is contained in NP; whether they are equal remains open.
Proof
Evidence sent to a verifier. In a zk-SNARK, this is a separate object generated using the private witness.
Prover / verifier
The party producing a proof / the party checking it.
Zero knowledge
The proof discloses nothing extra beyond what the public information and claim’s truth imply.
SNARK
Succinct Non-interactive Argument of Knowledge. Short proof, cheap verification, no proof-time dialogue, computational security, and a knowledge requirement.
zk-SNARK
A SNARK that also has the zero-knowledge privacy property.
Circuit / constraint
A computation expressed as small operations / a condition its values must satisfy.
Groth16
The proof system used by the Shielded Bitcoin example profile; it needs circuit-specific trusted setup.
Witness encryption
Encryption under a statement, with decryption enabled by a satisfying witness.

Definitions: Cook §§1–2; Thaler §§3, 7.4, 11.1; Groth; PIPEs §2.5.

Four questions to ask about any proof

  1. What exact claim is being proved?
  2. What evidence does the prover use?
  3. What does the verifier see and check?
  4. What assumptions and additional protocol checks are needed?

A presentation sentence

“The wallet proves that it knows private notes it can spend and that the new transfer obeys the rules. Indexers check that proof and the public replay rules. Bitcoin supplies publication and order.”

Shielded Bitcoin §§14–15.
